# LTLINE Financial Framework

**Document ID:** LTLINE-DOC-08  
**Revision:** 1.0  
**Status:** DRAFT

## 1. Purpose

This framework defines the management structure for budgeting, costing, cash flow, profitability and financial reporting.

## 2. Financial Classification

Every financial figure should be classified as:

- `ASSUMPTION` — planning estimate
- `QUOTE` — supplier/customer quoted value
- `ACTUAL` — realized transaction
- `FORECAST` — projected future value

## 3. Costing Model

For each product or project, distinguish:

**Direct Purchase Cost + Logistics / Landed Costs + Direct Labour + Other Direct Costs = Direct Cost**

Then:

**Selling Price − Direct Cost = Gross Contribution**

Operating expenses are tracked separately from direct project/product costs.

## 4. Budget Structure

The annual and monthly budget should cover:

- Revenue
- Cost of goods / materials
- Direct labour
- Gross contribution
- Operating expenses
- Taxes and statutory obligations
- Capital expenditure
- Financing costs where applicable
- Net cash movement

## 5. Cash Flow

Cash flow monitoring should distinguish:

- Customer receivables
- Supplier payables
- Inventory purchases
- Payroll
- Taxes
- Fixed operating expenses
- Capital expenditure
- Financing inflows / repayments

Profitability and cash availability must not be treated as the same measure.

## 6. Project Profitability

Each significant project should have a baseline budget and an actual-cost comparison. Changes, rework and unexpected costs should be recorded so that the final margin can be understood.

## 7. Inventory

Inventory records should track quantities, unit cost, total value and movement. Slow-moving or obsolete stock should be identified periodically.

## 8. Management Reporting

A monthly management report should ideally include:

- Sales
- Gross margin
- Operating expenses
- Cash position
- Receivables
- Payables
- Inventory
- Project pipeline
- Project profitability
- Major risks / deviations

## 9. Financial Approval

Spending authority should be defined by management. Significant purchases, unusual discounts, credit exposure and capital expenditure should require documented approval.

## 10. Data Integrity

Financial records should be supported by invoices, receipts, contracts, purchase orders, quotations or other appropriate evidence. Planning assumptions must remain visibly separated from actual accounting data.
